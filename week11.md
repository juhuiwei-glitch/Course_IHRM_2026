# Choosing Motivational Methods

<div class="case-credits">

Original Mandarin Chinese version by Professor Dah-Hsian William Seetoo

English translation by Dr. Ju-Hui Wei

</div>

<div class="case-intro" markdown="1">

Below are a number of motivational methods and profiles of several employees. Imagine that you are the manager of these employees. Which motivational method would you choose to improve each employee’s job performance? In other words, for each employee, select the one motivational method that you consider most appropriate and explain your reasoning.

**Note:** The primary goal of this decision is to improve employee productivity, not simply to make employees happier.

</div>

## Motivational Methods

<div class="methods-list" markdown="1">

1. A bonus system based on individual performance. The amount of the bonus depends entirely on the individual’s performance.
2. Publicly recognize and praise the employee’s achievements.
3. Increase the employee’s salary.
4. Provide symbols showing higher status, such as a more luxurious office, a new title, or a personal secretary.
5. Threaten the employee with demotion or dismissal in order to encourage greater effort.
6. A bonus system based on the performance of the team to which the employee belongs.
7. A bonus system based on the performance of the team led by the employee.
8. Job enrichment: increase the depth of the job and the employee’s level of responsibility.
9. Increase employee benefits.
10. Give the employee greater participation in management decisions.
11. Reduce supervision and give the employee greater freedom to make decisions and take action.
12. Change or improve the work environment.
13. Change the employee’s job or responsibilities, such as by transferring the employee to a different position, in order to increase work effort.
14. Provide opportunities for further education or professional development.
15. Help the employee see a promising future in the job, for example through encouragement or by outlining a future promotion path.
16. Other.

</div>

<div class="profile-number">02</div>
<div class="profile-card"><span class="profile-name">Olivia Ou</span> is the head nurse at a large public hospital. She is 29 years old, married, and has two children. She is currently pursuing a graduate degree as a part-time student while continuing to work. Most of the doctors at the hospital consider Olivia highly capable and very competent.</div>
</div>

<div class="profile-item">
<div class="profile-number">03</div>
<div class="profile-card"><span class="profile-name">Chris Chang</span> is the Vice President of Sales at a large department store chain. He is 57 years old and has been widowed for many years. He has three children; two are married, and the youngest is still in college. Chris has worked for the company for nine years. In addition to a high salary, he also receives an annual share of the company’s profits, a benefit available only to senior executives.</div>
</div>

<div class="profile-item">
<div class="profile-number">04</div>
<div class="profile-card"><span class="profile-name">Tony Tai</span> works for a large supermarket. He is 26 years old and has worked for the supermarket since he was 18, starting when it was still under its previous ownership, except for a three-year interruption due to military service. Tony performs very well at work, but because he is currently studying business administration in the evening program of a private university, he works only four hours a day on site. He is expected to graduate from university in six months.</div>
</div>

<div class="profile-item">
<div class="profile-number">05</div>
<div class="profile-card"><span class="profile-name">Grace Ge</span> is a product planning specialist in the product division of a large household products company. She assists the product manager in developing marketing strategies, coordinates with the manufacturing and sales departments, and occasionally visits distributors. Grace is 26 years old and unmarried. She is sharp-minded, highly driven, and recently returned from abroad after earning a master’s degree in business administration. She could be seen as a representative of a new generation of women.</div>
</div>

<div class="profile-item">
<div class="profile-number">06</div>
<div class="profile-card"><span class="profile-name">Howard Hung</span> is a first-line manager at a medium-sized company. He is 25 years old, unmarried, and graduated from a five-year junior college program. In addition to his salary, he receives investment income from an inheritance left by his father. This investment income is about twice his annual salary.</div>
</div>

<div class="profile-item">
<div class="profile-number">07</div>
<div class="profile-card"><span class="profile-name">Monica Ma</span> is an administrative assistant in a graduate program. She is 31 years old and married. After graduating from vocational high school, she completed one year of professional secretarial training and then joined the institution as a typist. Over the next 12 years, she advanced through several positions to her current role. Her responsibilities include providing students with information about course selection and degree requirements, assisting with course registration, maintaining academic records, and serving as a liaison among the graduate program, faculty members, and students. She also occasionally handles external communications. Some of the students she works with are working professionals enrolled in part-time graduate programs.</div>
</div>

<div class="profile-item">
<div class="profile-number">08</div>
<div class="profile-card"><span class="profile-name">Leo Lee</span> is a chemist in the research division of a large petrochemical company. He is 30 years old. He joined the company six years ago after completing his military service. Leo has made major contributions to the company’s research work, but he is not very good at communicating with others and does not appear to have strong potential in administrative work. In recent years, many of his fellow chemists with similar experience have taken on managerial responsibilities, while Leo has not been given such an opportunity. This has had a noticeable effect on his morale.</div>
</div>

<div class="profile-item">
<div class="profile-number">09</div>
<div class="profile-card"><span class="profile-name">Lily Lo</span> is 17 years old and began working at an electronics factory after graduating from junior high school. Her work is monotonous, and the pay is relatively low. Some coworkers from her hometown have left for jobs at other factories, and Lily has sometimes considered doing the same.</div>
</div>

<div class="profile-item">
<div class="profile-number">10</div>
<div class="profile-card"><span class="profile-name">Charlie Chao</span> graduated from the Department of International Trade at a national university. He is highly capable and has strong language skills. Charlie is 30 years old and has worked for a medium-sized trading company for six years. Recently, he seems less enthusiastic about handling assignments from his supervisors. The head of the company suspects that Charlie may be considering starting his own business.</div>
</div>

<div class="profile-item">
<div class="profile-number">11</div>

</div>

<div class="profile-item">
<div class="profile-number">12</div>

He has a distinguished academic reputation in Western history and has published several articles in international academic journals. He has also published a substantial scholarly book in Taiwan. In the past, Frank was highly dedicated to teaching and received consistently positive evaluations from students. However, over the past two years, his interest in and enthusiasm for teaching appear to have declined noticeably. He has also not published any academic work in the past four years. His salary has already reached the top of the pay scale for full professors at public universities.</div>
